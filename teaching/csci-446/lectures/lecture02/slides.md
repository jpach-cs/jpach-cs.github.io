# Intelligent Agents

In which we discuss the nature of agents, the diversity of environments, and various agent types.

---

# What is an Agent?

- **Definition**: An agent perceives its environment via **sensors** and acts upon it through **actuators**.
- **Observation**: Some systems, like flush toilets and calculators, seem like agents but are actually artifacts.

---

# Agents vs. Artifacts

- **Artifacts**: Devices like flush toilets perform actions based on simple rules, but do not adapt or learn.
- **Example**: Calculator reacts deterministically to inputs without decision-making capability.
- Insight: Important to distinguish between true agents and simple reactive systems.

---

# Rational Agents

- A rational agent does the "right thing."
- **Performance Measure**: Key to evaluating desirability of actions but can be challenging to define correctly.

---

# Importance of Environment

- **Task Environment**: Consists of the performance measure, environment, actuators, and sensors (PEAS).
- Types:
  - Fully vs. partially observable
  - Single-agent vs. multiagent
  - Deterministic vs. nondeterministic

---

# Types of Agents

Simple Reflex Agents

- **Behavior**: Act based on current percept only.
- **Example**: Basic vacuum agent.

Model-based Reflex Agents

- **Behavior**: Maintain internal state based on history of percepts.

Goal-based Agents

- **Behavior**: Actions achieve specified goals.

Utility-based Agents

- **Behavior**: Actions maximize perceived utility.

---

# Learning Agents

- Capabilities include learning from interactions to improve performance over time.
- **Components**:
  - **Performance Element**
  - **Learning Element**
  - **Critic**
  - **Problem Generator**

---

## Agent Design Considerations

- **Artifacts vs. Agents**: Distinguish design based on adaptability and decision-making capabilities.
- **Scalability and Flexibility**: Focus on building systems that can learn from experience and adjust accordingly.

---

# Design Considerations

- **Scalability and Flexibility**: Construct designs that can adapt to different and complex environments.
- Emphasize the need for agents to learn and adapt autonomously over time.

---

# Summary

- Understanding agents requires considering their capabilities, environment, and autonomy.
- Real-life applications necessitate a balance between predefined rules and adaptability.

---

# Conclusion

This chapter establishes a foundational understanding of intelligent agents, exploring their design, environment considerations, and types, as essential components of AI systems.
